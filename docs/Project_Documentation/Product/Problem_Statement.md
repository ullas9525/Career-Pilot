# Problem Statement

## Context
In the modern technical recruitment landscape, graduating engineering and management students face a significant gap between academic knowledge and industry expectations. While universities teach theoretical concepts and coding syntax, real-world technical interviews demand problem-solving under pressure, deep architectural understanding, and the ability to articulate trade-offs.

## The Core Problem
Students consistently fail early-stage technical interviews not because they lack technical ability, but because they lack **interview readiness and practice**. 

Traditional mock interviews are:
1. **Unscalable:** Faculty and placement cells do not have the time or specialized knowledge to conduct realistic, role-specific mock interviews for hundreds of students across diverse technical roles (Frontend, Backend, Data Science, Product Management, etc.).
2. **Expensive:** Hiring external industry experts for mock interviews is cost-prohibitive for most colleges.
3. **Inconsistent:** Human interviewers have biases, varying standards, and often fail to provide granular, actionable feedback.
4. **Intimidating:** Students experience severe anxiety during human mock interviews, preventing them from practicing consistently.

## The Consequence
Because students cannot practice effectively, they enter high-stakes placement interviews unprepared. This results in poor placement outcomes for the college, low confidence for the student, and wasted time for hiring companies.

## The Proposed Solution (CareerPilot)
There is a critical need for an automated, scalable, and zero-cost (at the margin) mock interview platform that simulates realistic, role-specific technical interviews. The solution must utilize advanced AI (LLMs and Speech-to-Text/Text-to-Speech) to conduct dynamic, conversational interviews based on real-world Job Descriptions (JDs) and the student's personal resume. It must provide brutal, objective, and dimension-specific scoring (e.g., Code Quality, System Design, Communication) immediately after the session, allowing students to iterate and improve rapidly without relying on human availability.
