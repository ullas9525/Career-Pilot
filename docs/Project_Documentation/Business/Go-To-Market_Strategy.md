# Go-To-Market Strategy

## 1. Product Positioning
CareerPilot is positioned not as an educational tool that teaches how to code, but as an **assessment and readiness platform** that bridges the gap between academic capability and industry employability. It is the "flight simulator" for technical interviews.

## 2. Launch Strategy (The Trojan Horse Approach)
Selling enterprise software to universities is notoriously slow. Therefore, the GTM strategy bypasses the initial procurement bottleneck through a free, data-driven pilot program.

### Phase 1: Free Beta Pilots (Months 1-3)
- **Target:** 3 to 5 Tier-2 engineering colleges.
- **Offer:** A free 3-month pilot for their final-year Computer Science students.
- **Execution:** Onboard 500+ students. Let them use the platform leading up to the initial campus placement drives.
- **Goal:** Gather massive amounts of usage data and prove that students who use CareerPilot score higher in actual interviews.

### Phase 2: The Data Reveal (Month 4)
- Present the Placement Coordinators with a detailed analytics report: "Students with a Career Readiness Score > 7.0 received job offers at a 40% higher rate than those below 4.0."
- Once the predictive power of the platform is demonstrated, the university is highly incentivized to formalize the relationship to boost their overall placement metrics.

### Phase 3: Paid Licensing (Month 5+)
- Transition the pilot colleges to paid annual licenses (e.g., $15,000/year for unlimited campus usage).
- Use the case studies and data generated from Phase 1 to sell the product to neighboring institutions.

## 3. Marketing Channels
- **Direct B2B Outreach:** Cold emailing/LinkedIn outreach to University Deans, Directors of Training and Placement, and Heads of Computer Science Departments.
- **Webinars / Whitepapers:** Publishing data on "The State of Engineering Employability" using anonymized data collected during the mock interviews.
- **Student Advocacy:** While the product is B2B, creating a product so good that students demand it from their placement cells (bottom-up pressure).
